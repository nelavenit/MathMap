**Intuition.** Imagine an infinite hotel, all the rooms are numbered $1,2,3,\ldots$ with no upper limit. Initially every room is occupied, but new guests keep coming and are expecting their own room. A normal, finite hotel can't accommodate new guests once every room is full. However the Grand Hotel can:
1. If there are finitely many new guests, say $k$, we can just ask every guest from the room number $n$ to move to a room number $n+k$, for each single guest this is a finite process to walk to the room just $k$ doors away.
2. If there are a countable number of guests, we can ask every guest to move to the room of twice the number $n \mapsto 2$n$, and put new guests in now empty rooms of uneven numbers.
This contradicts the life intuition: "full = can't put any more".
