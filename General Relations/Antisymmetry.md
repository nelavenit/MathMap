**Definition.** Let $A$ be a set and $\circ$ be a binary relation on $A$. Then $\circ$ is called <u>antisymmetric</u> if $\forall a_1, a_2 \in A$ we have $a_1 \circ a_2$ and $a_2 \circ a_1$ implies $a_1 = a_2$. (In other words, $a_1 \neq a_2$ implies that at maximum one of the $a_1 \circ a_2$ and $a_2 \circ a_1$ may be right)

**Remark.** Being antisymmetric is neither a [[Symmetry|non-symmetric]], nor is stronger than being non-symmetric as in the case of [[Reflexivity|reflexivity]] and [[Irreflexivity|irreflexivity]]. The relation can be both symmetric and antisymmetric (see example (4) and [[Symmetric Antisymmetric Relations|here]] is their full description).

**Examples.** Consider two-point set $\{(0,1)\}$:
1. $\emptyset$ is antisymmetric
2. $\{(0,1)\}$ is antisymmetric
3. $\{(0,1), (1,0)\}$ is non-antisymmetric and symmetric
