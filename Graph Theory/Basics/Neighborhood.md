**Definition.** Let $\mathbb{G} = (G, E)$ be a [[Graph|graph]], $v \in G$. The set of vertices adjacent to $v$ – $\{ u \in G\ |\ (v, u) \in E\}$ – is called an <u>open neighborhood of </u>$v$ and is denoted by $N_{\mathbb{G}}(v)$ (or $N(v)$ for brevity, if $\mathbb{G}$ is unambiguous from the context). 

**Definition.** Let $\mathbb{G} = (G, E)$ be a [[Graph|graph]], $v \in G$. The set $N(v) \cup \{ v \}$ – is called a <u>closed neighborhood of </u>$v$ and is denoted by $N_{\mathbb{G}}[v]$ (or $N[v]$ for brevity, if $\mathbb{G}$ is unambiguous from the context). 

**Definition.** Let $\mathbb{G} = (G, E)$ be a graph, $S \subseteq G$. Then $\bigcup\limits_{s \in S}{N(s)}$ is called a <u>neighborhood of </u>$S$, and is denoted by $N_{\mathbb{G}}(S)$ (or $N(S)$ for brevity, if $\mathbb{G}$ is unambiguous from the context).

**Definition.** Let $\mathbb{G} = (G, E)$ be a graph, $S \subseteq G$. Then $\bigcup\limits_{s \in S}{N[s]} = N(S) \cup S$ is called a <u>closed neighborhood of </u>$S$, and is denoted by $N_{\mathbb{G}}[S]$ (or $N[S]$ for brevity, if $\mathbb{G}$ is unambiguous from the context).

**Definition.** Elements of $N(v)$ are called <u>neighbors of </u>$v$, elements of N(S) are called <u>neighbors of </u>$S$.
