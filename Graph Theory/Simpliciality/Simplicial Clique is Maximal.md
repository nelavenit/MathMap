**Statement.** Let $\mathbb{G} = (G, E)$ be a [[Graph|graph]], $v$ be a [[Simplicial Vertices and Cliques|simplicial vertex]]. Then the simplicial clique $N[v]$ is the unique maximal clique containing $v$.
$\blacktriangle$ Let $C$ be any clique of $\mathbb{G}$ containing $v$. For every $u \in C$, either $u=v$ or $(v,u) \in E$\)$. Hence $C \subseteq N[v]$ bu teh definition of a [[Neighborhood of Vertex|neighborhood]]. Since $N[v]$ itself is a clique containing $v$, it is the [[Least and Greatest Elements|greatest]] such clique under inclusion, and therefore the unique maximal clique containing $v$. $\boxtimes$

**Corollary 2.** Let $\mathbb{G} = (G, E)$ be a [[Graph|graph]] and $C$ be a [[Simplicial Vertices and Cliques|simplicial clique]] of $\mathbb{G}$. Then $C$ is maximal.
$\color{red}\text{Proof}$ is a trivial exercise to follow the definition of a simplicial clique.
