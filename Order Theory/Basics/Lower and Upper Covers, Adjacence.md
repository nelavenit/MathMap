**Definition.** Let $(P,\leq)$ be a [[Partial Order|poset]]. Then $p \in P$ is called a <u>lower cover</u> of $q \in P$ (and $q$ is called an <u>upper cover</u> of $p$) iff $p<q$ and $∀z∈P$ we have that $p≤z≤q$ implies $z∈\{p,q\}$. We write p $\prec$ q . 

**Definition.** Points $p,q$ that satisfy either p $\prec$ q or q $\prec$ p are called <u>adjacent</u>.

**Examples.** 
1. In every finite order every element either is maximal or has an upper cover.
2. In $\mathbb{Q}$ with a natural ordering every element has no upper and no lover covers.

**Statement.** Being a lower cover and being an upper cover are [[Dual Properties|dual properties]].
$\color{red}\text{Proof}$ is a trivial exercise.

**Statement.** Being adjacent is a [[Self-dual Properties|self-dual]] property of a pair of order elements .
$\color{red}\text{Proof}$ is a trivial exercise.
