**Definition.** Let $A$ be a set and $\mathrel{R}$ be a [[Relation and Relational Structure|binary relation]] on $A$. Then $\mathrel{R}$ is called <u>irreflexive</u> if $\forall a \in A$ we have $\lnot (a \mathrel{R} a)$.

**Remark.** Being irreflexive is not a negation of being [[Reflexivity|reflexive]], it's stronger than just non-reflexive. Relations can be both non-reflexive and non-irreflexive.

**Examples.** Consider two-point set $\{0,1\}$:
1. $\{(0,1)\}$ is irreflexive
2. $\{(0,0)\}$ is neither irreflexive, nor reflexive
