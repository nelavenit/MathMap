**Definition.** Let $A$ be a set and $\mathrel{R}$ be a [[Relation and Relational Structure|binary relation]] on $A$. Then $\mathrel{R}$ is called <u>reflexive</u> if $\forall a \in A$ we have $a \mathrel{R} a$.

**Remark.** Being reflexive is not a negation of being [[Irreflexivity|irreflexive]], it's stronger than just non-irreflexive. Relations can be both non-reflexive and non-irreflexive.

**Examples.** Consider two-point set $\{0,1\}$:
1. $\{(0,0), (1,1)\}$ is reflexive
2. $\{(0,0)\}$ is neither reflexive, nor irreflexive
3. $=$ is reflexive on any set
