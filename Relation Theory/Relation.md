**Definition.** Let $A_1,\ldots,A_n$ be sets, where $n\geq1$. A subset $R\subseteq A_1\times\cdots\times A_n$ is called an $n$-<u>ary relation</u> between_ $A_1,\ldots,A_n$. If $A_1=\cdots=A_n=A$, it is called an $n$<u>-ary relation</u> on $A$. 

**Definition.** Let $R$ be a $2$-ary relation on $A$, then it is called a <u>binary relation</u> and $(a_1, a_2) \in R$ is denoted by $a_1 \mathrel{R} a_2$. 

**Definition.** Let $R$ be a $1$-ary relation on $A$, then it is called an <u>unary relation</u>.

**Definition.** Let $R$ be an $n$-ary relation, then $n$ is called an <u>arity of </u>$R$. 

**Definition.** Fix an indexed family of relation symbols $(R_i)_{i\in I}$, where $R_i$ has arity $n_i$. This information is called a <u>relational signature</u>. A <u>relational structure</u> of this signature is a tuple $\mathbb A=\bigl(A,(R_i^{\mathbb A})_{i\in I}\bigr)$, where $R_i^{\mathbb A}\subseteq A^{n_i}.$

The set $A$ is called its _underlying set_ or _domain_.
